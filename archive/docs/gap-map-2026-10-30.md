# Gap map of the decode layer, 2026-10-30 (after 1.930x)

Read-only analysis of main at 6944c2a. No code changed and no check was run for it. It
refreshes [gap-map-2026-10-29.md](gap-map-2026-10-29.md) for the current layer and keeps its
method, with the two corrections appended to that file: the "time between kernels" residual is
mostly single-sample row variance and PDL accounting, not idle time, and its E2 premise (f16 P.V
in mode 51) was wrong.

## Inputs and method

- **Ours.** The scored run 0fae646889864106 (1.930x, CI 1.901-1.959, main 7dde781, MoE mode 28
  "main-lean" at 32 / 128 / 256 tokens) and the scored run before it, 5bb37c21c13245b8 (1.908x,
  745021d, mode 26 "main-dynb-fs" at the same sizes). Per-shape medians, profile rows, the 20-60
  per-run times and the TL timeline lines come from
  `muse_decode_layer/profiles/submission.<job>.{json,log}`; `okc status 0fae646889864106 --json`
  agrees (status done, board muse_decode_layer).
- **Which run per load.** Between the two runs only the MoE fat path changed, and only the 32,
  128 and 256 token loads use it. For decode 1, decode 16 and verify 1 I use the mean of both
  runs (same code, two samples); for decode 32, verify 16 and verify 32 I use 0fae64 alone.
- **Public.** The judged in-layer profile of the public entry's job f662ee18 (2.092x), as in
  [gap-analysis-2026-10-28.md](gap-analysis-2026-10-28.md) section 2: attention, MoE, dense
  (qkv, o, pooler with the router, unpooler) and glue (two boundaries and a route kernel that
  also does the pre-expert norm).
- **Score math.** Per load, C = 46 t_sliding + 16 t_full; the score is the geometric mean of
  the six ratios C_sglang / C_ours. A load's share of the remaining gap is ln(C_ours / C_public)
  over the sum of that log across loads; closing a load lifts the score by
  ln(C_ours / C_public) / 6. Same script as the 10-29 map.
- **Caveats carried over.** Public times come from another judge run (plus or minus 3-5% per
  shape). Under PDL a secondary kernel's row includes its wait on the predecessor, so the public
  rows overstate its later kernels; only component plus residual compares like for like. A
  profile row is one input; the judged time is the median over 20 (60 at decode 1) fresh
  inputs, so a row can sit 10-15 us from the median on its own.

## 3. Five experiments by expected value

Expected value is the predicted score gain times the chance the mechanism is real, over the
cost of the cheapest decisive test. Score percentages use the per-microsecond table in section
0. Already in flight and not ranked: the ONE_PFAST layer port (f2d4eb5); transferring its
standalone judged deltas (verify 16 / 32 full -5.8 / -12.3, decode 16 / 32 full -2.6 / -10.4)
predicts about +0.5% on the layer (inferred).

### E1. Measure, then close, the MoE gap at 1, 8 and 16 tokens

- **Bottleneck.** Verify 1 carries the largest share (21.5%) and its gap is the MoE (+8.2 us
  weighted, cross-run). No same-process comparison of our skinny kernel (sk8w4s) against the
  public MoE kernel exists below 32 tokens; every A/B (d1f587760b1241dc, 4c285df44eb34bc1,
  70dd7b13be884434) ran 32, 128 and 256.
- **Why a gap is expected (inferred).** The public MoE board entry is 2.327x against our 2.149x
  (f928f8a802014e4f). The same-process gaps at 32 / 128 / 256 tokens explain 0.23 of the 0.48
  summed-log difference; the other 0.25 falls on the three small sizes, about 8% each if spread
  evenly (bs1 28.0, bs8 68.5, bs16 96.0 us standalone). Cross-run and assumption-laden: plus or
  minus 3% per size.
- **Mechanism (hypothesis, to be named by the test).** The public kernel at 16 tokens and below
  runs one persistent launch with routing in every CTA, whole 36 KB units by one bulk copy into a
  3-slot ring consumed through registers, the first units issued before routing, and K split
  across the 8 warps with an smem reduction (gap analysis 3.2). Which of those our skinny path
  lacks at these sizes is the open question.
- **Predicted saving.** 8% of our layer MoE rows: about 2.3 us at decode 1, 4.4 at verify 1, 6 at
  decode 16. Score +1.0% to +1.7% if fully closed (inferred).
- **Why ruled-out.md does not close it.** Its MoE rows are about the fat path (partitioning,
  staged pair, ring releases), per-expert tile size, no K split, layout tweaks, and TMA weights
  (M2), which lost at 1-32 tokens feeding an smem pipeline to mma.sync and was reopened on
  2026-10-28 with the public bulk-copy-to-registers mechanism, never rebuilt. None compares the
  skinny path to the public kernel.
- **Cheapest decisive test.** One MoE bench check: `tools/moe_make_bench_ab.py` with the public
  MoE file as an arm (loaded from the session scratchpad, not committed, as in
  d1f587760b1241dc), sizes 1, 8 and 16, six seeds of the layer's judge-like routing, cold L2
  rows, plus the public kernel's OKC_DEBUG stamps against our skinny stamps. Stop if the public
  arm leads by under 3% at 8 tokens; otherwise the stamps name the phase to port.

### E2. One main-stream route kernel before the MoE, and the MoE as its PDL secondary

- **Bottleneck (measured).** At 128 tokens the main stream finishes the pre-expert norm and the
  MoE then waits 3.7-4.1 us for the side stream's hi + lo add and routing (four timelines). At 32
  tokens the wait is 1.5 us. Inside the MoE, consumers start 10.9 us after launch against 6.2
  for the public kernel (stamps, 70dd7b13be884434), of which routing is 3.6 against 2.5.
- **Mechanism.** The side stream existed to hide the router GEMM under the pooler. The router now
  lives inside the front launch at 8-256 tokens (skf, concat), so the side stream carries only 1-5
  us of elementwise and routing work with nothing to hide under. Replace the fork, the add,
  SGLang's routing kernel, the pre-expert norm and the join with one kernel on the main stream,
  one program per token like the public "route" kernel: hi + lo add, sigmoid, bias, top-8, local
  ids, pre-expert norm, and the per-expert counts and offsets that mode 28 now rebuilds in its
  prologue (the 10-29 map's E1 moved into this kernel instead of onto a side stream). Then
  launch the MoE as that kernel's PDL secondary with an independent preamble before
  `griddepcontrol.wait`: barrier and ring initialisation, and an L2 prefetch of each local
  expert's first weight unit, a hint only, so correctness does not depend on routing. This is
  the single named PDL boundary: route kernel to MoE.
- **Predicted saving (hypothesis).** 3-5 us at 128 and 256 tokens (the join plus part of the
  setup), 0.5-1.5 us at 8, 16 and 32. Score +0.5% to +0.9%.
- **Why ruled-out.md does not close it.** The residual row (181ab39f1bd84912) closes the residual
  as idle time but names this join as open. The PDL row closes launch latency in our chain
  (weight prologues of bandwidth-bound projections did not shrink the step); this boundary's
  preamble is latency-bound setup the MoE serialises today, and the fork and join removal was
  never tested. The router rows are about the router GEMM's speed. The public entry's loss
  "routing fused into the pooler kernel" fused routing into a GEMM; this keeps it separate.
- **Cheapest decisive test.** One layer check with the route kernel as a front-tuner variant
  (`_front_tune` already times whole front chains at each token count), with a self-test that
  its top-8 ids equal SGLang's routing kernel on every tie case the reference allows, and the TL
  timeline printed for verify 16. Stop if the chain from the front GEMM's end to the MoE's
  start saves under 2 us at 128 tokens. The PDL preamble is a second check only after that.

### E3. The public GEMV geometry at 1 token (qkv, o, unpooler slice)

- **Bottleneck (cross-run).** At decode 1 our in-layer qkv / o / unpooler rows are 11.1 / 6.0 /
  4.5 us against public in-layer rows of 9.2 / 5.2 / 3.5, which include PDL waits and so overstate
  the public kernels. Our standalone sk qkv ran 9.1 us of kernel time (runs.md,
  3843af6418a84a72), but the layer's SKP tune picked cgemv for qkv at m = 1 (14.9 us against
  19.8 for proj_fwd, runs-decode-layer.md, the 1.643x row). Hypothesis: the large-smem sk kernels
  pay an L1/shared carveout switch inside the layer chain (runs.md names that mechanism,
  unmeasured).
- **Mechanism (hypothesis).** The public `cgemv` streams 12-bit weights with `__ldg` straight to
  registers and issues every load before the first FMA, and its in-layer bars match our
  standalone kernel times, consistent with no carveout cost; our cgemv runs 2,048 CTAs of 544
  threads in 5.2 waves.
- **Predicted saving.** 1.5-3.6 us at decode 1 (both shapes). Score +0.3% to +0.7%.
- **Why ruled-out.md does not close it.** The cgemv row closes our geometry (2 rows per CTA) on
  qkv and the pooler at m = 1 in the standalone; the public geometry was never timed. The L2
  prefetch rows warm L2 beside attention; this changes the kernel.
- **Cheapest decisive test.** One proj bench check with `tools/proj_make_bench_ab.py`: the public
  m = 1 GEMVs (scratchpad arm) against ours on qkv_gate, o and the unpooler slice, judge-style
  timing with the flush. Stop if the public arm leads by under 1 us on qkv.

### E4. The flat q_len 8 consumer path before the Q.K issue

- **Bottleneck (measured, same process).** After ONE_PFAST, mode 51 still trails the public flat
  kernel by about 20 us cold at verify 32 full and 11 at verify 16 full (bench 1e6626a427454f9a
  minus the judged PFAST step). The no-copy probe puts the whole gap in the instruction streams;
  the consumer path from the full-barrier wait to the Q.K issue is 94 instructions against 37.
- **Mechanism.** Carry the wgmma descriptors in uniform registers across tiles instead of
  rebuilding them per tile, and test "this tile is patched" with one compare as the public
  `r_lo` / `r_span` does. The per-tile `fence.proxy.async` stays (CLAUDE.md). Second lead, from
  the stamps: the two CTAs of one SM finish up to 27 us apart with equal tile counts, so a
  partition weighted per SM slot is the next candidate if the first does not close it.
- **Predicted saving (hypothesis).** 3-8 us at verify 32 full and 2-4 at verify 16 full. Score
  +0.06% to +0.15% on the layer, and the same shapes count on the attention board.
- **Why ruled-out.md does not close it.** The attention rows close the spilling W2 build, QKF,
  EARLY, the once-path diet, the P.V operand type, KVFAST, cross-tile pipelining and the unroll
  as explanations, and gather rate or DRAM traffic, the cooperative attribute and the consumer
  fence as pieces. The descriptor and patch-test path is listed as untested in runs-attention.md.
- **Cheapest decisive test.** One attention bench check with `tools/attn_make_bench_ab.py --shapes
  full`: a descriptor-carry arm against the board build, cold rows at verify 16 / 32 full, ABCHECK
  against the FP32 reference. Stop if the cold saving at verify 32 full is under 3 us.

### E5. One-launch concat front on the wide kernel at 128 tokens

- **Bottleneck (measured, our runs).** At 128 tokens the front is the cuBLAS concat GEMM (pooler +
  router hi + lo, 4,608 x 8,192, 26.1 us) plus a separate hi + lo add (1.8 us). In this run's
  tuner the wide 8-stage kernel beat cuBLAS on the pooler alone, 28.8 against 32.3 us
  (`SUMMARY proj pooler T=128`), but the "wide" front variant (wide pooler plus a separate
  router) lost to concat, 42.4 against 39.3.
- **Mechanism.** Run the concat weight through the wide kernel in one launch (b1n128c2s8h
  geometry, FP32 output for the router rows), with hi and lo rows interleaved so one CTA owns
  both halves of each router column and sums them in the epilogue, which also deletes the add
  kernel. At 256 tokens the wide family lost (8-stage and BN 256 rows), so 128 only.
- **Predicted saving (hypothesis).** The pooler-alone ratio (0.89) on 26.1 us plus the add: 2-4
  us at verify 16. Score +0.1% to +0.2%. If E2 lands first, the add is already gone and the
  saving is the GEMM part only.
- **Why ruled-out.md does not close it.** The wide-router row says "a wide concat GEMM is
  untested"; the declined separate-pooler row and the CUTLASS concat row are other kernels.
- **Cheapest decisive test.** A `wideconcat` variant in `_front_tune` at 128 tokens; one layer
  check, read the `TUNE front T=128` line. Stop if it is not at least 2 us under concat there.

### Considered and not ranked

- **Overlapping the router or routing with attention.** Not possible: the router input is
  h = RMS(stream1) x (1 + pre_ffn_norm) with stream1 the attention boundary's output
  (`ref_front` in `reference.py`), so routing depends on attention. The same holds for the
  MoE against the next layer's front, which is outside one layer call.
- **Fusing the attention boundary into the o projection epilogue.** The boundary needs each
  token's full 8,192-column RMS, so the o kernel would need a per-token ticket and a last-CTA
  finalize. The public entry measured that class losing (its o launch went from 9 to 49 us with a
  last-CTA glue tail), at 1 and 8 tokens our fastest boundary is an 8-CTA cluster per token that
  a one-CTA tail would replace, and at 128 and 256 tokens o runs on cuBLAS with no epilogue
  access (the wide o lost by 0.3 us, a candidate only if fused). Ceiling about 2-3 us per shape
  at 16-32 tokens (+0.4% to +0.6%), chance low, and the decisive test needs the fused kernel
  itself; a timing-only chain arm that skips the boundary would bound it in one check first.
- **Reducing MoE run-to-run variance from routing.** The per-run data argue against it. Within a
  run our 20 times correlate with SGLang's at r = 0.66-0.99 on every shape but decode 32 full,
  and the score uses paired ratios, so shared variation cancels. Regressing our per-run time on
  SGLang's gives slopes of 0.27-0.37 at 128 and 256 tokens against a mean ratio of 0.50-0.55, so
  there we are less sensitive than SGLang; at decode 1, decode 16 and verify 1 the slopes are
  0.48-0.84 against mean ratios of 0.47-0.56, a modest excess, and at decode 32 they do not
  exceed the ratio. Decode 1 shows the same 4.3 us spread on both sides with one token
  and fixed routing, so much of that variation is not routing. Between runs the judge holds
  each load's weighted MoE load within 1.5% of a target. The lever left is our time on the
  heavier draws at 8-32 tokens, which is E1's kernel work.
