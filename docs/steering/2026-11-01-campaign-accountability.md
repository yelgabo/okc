**Where I followed the guidance.** The paste ranked whole-layer low-token fusion first. Check `01183c2992eb4153` collected owned and SGLang timelines on all twelve layer shapes. On the two decode-one samples top-k finished 11.8 and 11.2 µs before pooler normalization. That deprioritizes speeding up top-k alone. Several proposed attention fusions were already implemented. P0 remained incomplete for physical bytes and per-kernel occupancy. My first timeline exceeded the log limit, and I discarded routing counts sampled before replay in the corrected run. The judge denied access to the hardware counter image in `04f0339fa9f248a0`. [Timeline evidence and limitations](2026-11-01-fusion-opportunity-audit.md).

**Where I departed from it.** The paste asks for roughly 2% full-layer time reduction or a credible route to another gain of that size. I applied numerical correctness and noise gates carefully but applied the opportunity-size gate too late. For example, recovering the entire observed QKV128 gap would have meant about 0.19–0.25% on the projection score. I still spent two projection checks isolating scheduling and epilogue differences. At the layer level I pursued a short-boundary change through multiple checks. It improved the affected loads, but the all-load factors were 1.003428 and 1.001829, with only one seed clearing its noise threshold. I reported many such milestones. Their value was narrowing hypotheses and preserving correctness. Their cumulative demonstrated score gain was small. The broader expert-output/unpooler/final-boundary pipeline and the QKV-to-attention projection boundary remain open architectural questions. [Projection decision](2026-11-01-short-boundary.md) and [layer confirmation](2026-11-01-matched-short-boundary.md).

**Decision rule if work resumes.** Before launching another layer experiment, write down the affected official loads, required microseconds, and the specific work the implementation could remove while preserving precision and producer/consumer ordering. At the scored layer receipt `8f2780d7382047e6`, the context-weighted mean times are 78.334 µs for decode1 and 117.670 µs for verify1. With the other four loads fixed, saving 5.524 µs on each of those two loads would reduce geometric-mean latency by 2%. This is a break-even target, not a predicted speedup. First evaluate whether a combined expert-output/unpooler/final-boundary design can expose that much time. Include the required BF16 aggregate rounding, the existing short-unpool optimization and the cost of cross-block reductions. If the time budget cannot support it, pivot to a change covering more loads. For standalone boards, apply the same arithmetic using their own scoring rules. Limit smaller experiments to ones that decide a named larger design.
