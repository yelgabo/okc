## Arithmetic contract

At4284, `fork_bf16(v)` is `__bfloat162float(__float2bfloat16_rn(v))`. Let d be that required down-result BF16 boundary and g the required `BF16(1+rawBF16)` boundary. The incumbent stores FP32 RN(d*g); the candidate stores d exactly and uses explicit `__fmul_rn` at5868 after loading d. A finite BF16 value is exactly representable in FP32, and converting it back to BF16 is idempotent, including signed zero and subnormal encodings under preserving conversion semantics. A product of two BF16 significands fits exactly in binary64; the CPU model's final f32 conversion therefore supplies a useful RN model, including product underflow/overflow. It is not a CUDA implementation test.

The explicit multiply prevents contraction with subsequent multiply-adds across the former FP32 store boundary. Normalization reduction order is source-identical. B4 continues to compute its partials from the original gamma products; ordinary compiler FMA contraction within the unchanged square/add tree is not newly authorized reassociation. Target codegen must still confirm those boundaries and reduction behavior. Negative zero is preserved by the CPU roundtrip. Finite raw gamma -1 yields zero, its closest larger neighbor yields2^-8, and product subnormals remain reachable even though subnormal effective gamma does not. Mixed flush-to-zero behavior can distinguish the implementations, but no such actual flag difference was found. The extra BF16 conversion and packed decode need target confirmation for exceptional NaNs; Python struct and the original test's NaN helper are not evidence of CUDA payload/signaling preservation.
