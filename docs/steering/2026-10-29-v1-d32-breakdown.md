## 4. Where the remaining time is, structurally

At 8 tokens every kernel but the MoE is latency-bound. The four skinny GEMV launches run 13.6 us
above their streaming time at 4.05 TB/s (qkv +3.3, o +3.2, front +4.2, slice +2.9), the three
glue kernels are 11-12 us of pure latency, and the TL timelines of this run show 0.4-0.7 us
gaps at each of the 8 boundaries. That is roughly 30 of 118.6 us that are ramp, tail or
dependency latency, not bytes (arithmetic from the rows, labelled as such). At 32 tokens the
same items are about 19 us of GEMV excess and 12.5 us of glue on a 168 us step.

The layer's PDL map at T <= 32 (from the source at `efddc84`): attention launches plain
(`_ONE_PDL = False`; its two inserted `griddepcontrol.wait` lines are then no-ops) and never
executes `griddepcontrol.launch_dependents`; both attention-boundary kernels and the FFN
boundary trigger at entry; the MoE is the route kernel's PDL secondary; the four sk projections
launch plain (`tools/dl_proj_inline.py`: "the kernel source ... has no griddepcontrol and the
layer launches it plain").

## 5. Ranked mechanisms

### M1. Stream the o projection's weights during attention (PDL at the attention -> o boundary)

- **Mechanism.** Launch the sk o projection as a PDL secondary and have attention trigger its
  dependents at entry. The o CTAs then become resident on the SMs attention leaves idle (33 of
  132 busy at verify 1 sliding) and issue their weight bulk copies before
  `griddepcontrol.wait`; only x (attention's output) waits. The same switch applies to the
  front after the attention boundary, which already triggers at entry.
- **Why it can be large at 8 tokens (arithmetic from `SK_GEOM`).** o8 is R = 32 rows per CTA
  over K = 1024: 4 stages of 4 units, and the ring has 4 stages, so the whole 48 KB weight slice
  of each of the 256 CTAs fits before the wait (100% of o's 12.6 MB). o16 and o32 hold 3 of 4
  stages (75%); the slice at 8 tokens holds 100% but follows the persistent MoE, so it can only
  use SMs whose MoE CTA has exited; the fronts hold 3 of 16 pooler stages (about 19%).
- **Predicted saving (hypothesis).** o row at verify 1 sliding 6.3 us, streaming time 3.1, so
  after the wait it needs x (16 KB), the MMAs and the epilogue: -2 to -3.5 us at verify 1
  sliding, -0.5 to -2 at verify 1 full (mode 11 occupies most SMs), -1 to -2.5 at decode 32
  sliding and -0.5 to -1.5 at full (mode 49 runs 264 resident CTAs), plus up to about 2 us of
  the front's prefill under the boundary. Score: verify 1 +0.2% to +0.5%, decode 32 +0.07% to
  +0.2%, decode 16 about +0.1% to +0.25%; **+0.4% to +0.9% in all** (hypothesis).
- **Why ruled-out.md does not close it.** The PDL row (`179cecdac87b4872`, `e8b74a3519a94bc6`)
  closed launch latency in the captured graph with the old `proj_kernel` (mma.sync, cp.async)
  streaming its first three stages before the wait. At that time o at 8 tokens ran on cuBLAS
  (gap-analysis section 3.3: "our kernel only where it won at m = 8 (all but o)"), so the o
  boundary never had a kernel that could prefill, and attention was a multi-launch chain. New
  evidence: the sk o ring holds the whole CTA slice at 8 tokens (above), the attention -> o
  boundary is now one attention launch followed directly by the sk o kernel, and the public
  entry measured PDL with sk2 weight stages before the wait as a win at T <= 32 (its decode-layer
  memo, quoted in [audits-2026-10-28/public.md](../audits-2026-10-28/public.md)). The two L2
  prefetch rows (`985732949c07474f`, `34568f495b024c2f`) streamed 34 MB on a side stream into
  L2 and slowed attention; this moves at most 12.6 MB into the consumer's own shared memory.
- **Risks.** HBM contention with attention on the full shapes (the prefetch rows saw attention
  +0.8 us beside a 34 MB stream); co-residency of o CTAs (about 78 KB shared memory, 288
  threads, 68-70 registers) beside each attention instance is not computed for every mode.
- **Cheapest decisive test.** One layer check of the bundle with `SK_PDL=1` and `ONE_TRIG=1`
  compiled in, the o (and front) `sk_fwd` / `skf_fwd` calls at T <= 32 with bit 16 of `cfg` set
  as a front-tuner style chain variant, and the chain attention -> o -> boundary timed both ways
  at 8, 16 and 32 tokens as cold-L2 graph replays in prepare, plus the TL timeline at verify 1
  and decode 32. **Stop rule:** if the chain saves under 1.0 us at 8 tokens sliding, close it
  in `docs/ruled-out.md`, scoped to ring prefill at the attention -> o boundary. Built (section
  6).

### M2. MoE at 32 tokens: measure it, then decide on asymmetry

- **Bottleneck.** The one same-process MoE gap left after the bundle is at 32 tokens, about
  3.6 us inferred (3.6 x 0.080% = +0.29% if closed), and the unexplained 7 us of decode 32 sits
  in the MoE row.
- **What would justify an asymmetric T32 design.** A same-process bench at 32 tokens on layer
  routing (arms: 37, 42 for stamps, sk8w3s, the public MoE with its DEBUG stamps; seeds 1-6)
  showing at least 3 us of the remaining gap in phase A or B work on at least 5 of 6 seeds.
  The mode-23 stamps put the 32-token gap in setup (+7.2) and tail (+4.8), with phase A +2.3
  and phase B +0.8 (`d1f587760b1241dc`), and modes 26 / 28 have since cut the setup. If the
  remainder is still setup and tail, asymmetric GEMM tiling is the wrong lever; the audit's
  deferral stands and the fix belongs in the fat kernel's routing and tail.
- **Why ruled-out.md does not close it.** The staged pair row closed two-launch GEMM1/GEMM2 at
  128-256 tokens only. Nothing measured the public kernel at 32 tokens after mode 23.
- **Cheapest decisive test.** One MoE check:
  `tools/moe_make_bench_ab.py --base=37 --sizes=32 --routings=layer` with o42 and sk8w3s arms
  and the public file loaded from the session scratchpad. This also settles whether sk8w3s
  should enter the T = 32 layer pick.

### M3. Fold the route kernel into the MoE at T <= 32 (route -> MoE boundary)

- **Mechanism (hypothesis).** The route kernel runs 4.4-4.9 us for 8-32 tokens (top-8 of 256 and
  an 8192-wide norm per token); its work could run as the MoE prologue in every CTA, deleting one
  launch and its boundary. Predicted 1.5-3 us per shape at 8 and 32 tokens (+0.3% to +0.6%
  across verify 1, decode 32 and decode 16).
- **Against it.** The public entry keeps a separate route kernel ahead of its persistent MoE,
  and it measured a boundary prologue that recomputes the norm in every CTA losing 13.3 us at
  T = 1. Ranked third for that reason.
- **Why ruled-out.md does not close it.** E2 adopted the route kernel and nothing tested routing
  inside the MoE; the public's "routing fused into the pooler kernel" loss is a different
  boundary.
- **Cheapest decisive test.** Needs layer and MoE changes together; not built here.

Not ranked: the attention q_len 1 sliding gap (mode 1, +2.0 us at decode 32, +1.3 at decode 16,
same plan and fewer instructions than the public kernel, KVFAST null) is worth +0.2% if closed
but has no named mechanism yet; the residual at verify 1 needs the same-process whole-layer
comparison the audit already reserves (layer checks 2-3), which should also print the TL
timeline at verify 1 so per-kernel rows can be compared on one input.

## 6. Built: `SK_PDL` and `ONE_TRIG` (compile-gated, off by default)

Files (neither is owned by the layer agent):

- `muse_bf16_proj/submission.py`, `_SK_SRC`: `SK_PDL` (default 0). With 1, every single-CTA
  sk config initialises its barriers, issues the weight (and fused router-piece) bulk copies of
  the first `min(NSTAGE, stages)` stages with each stage's full `expect_tx`, loads the escape
  table, then every thread executes `griddepcontrol.wait`, then thread 0 issues those stages' x
  TMA copies; the producer warp continues from stage `npre`. Each full barrier still expects
  weights plus x, so it cannot complete before x lands. `sk_fwd` / `skf_fwd` take bit 16 of
  `cfg` as "launch with `cudaLaunchAttributeProgrammaticStreamSerialization`"; a file-scope flag
  carries it to `launch_sk`, so the dispatch lines `tools/dl_proj_inline.py` generates stay
  valid. Cluster configs (the standalone router) keep the old path.
- `muse_attention_decode/attn_one.cu`: `ONE_TRIG` (default 0). With 1, the q_len 1 and batch-1
  q_len 8 instances (the plans whose layer successor is the sk o projection) execute
  `griddepcontrol.launch_dependents` at entry. A plain successor ignores it; a PDL successor
  reads attention's output only after `griddepcontrol.wait`, which waits for the grid's
  completion and memory flush (PTX ISA 9.7.15.14). No fence, barrier or arrive moves in either
  file.

Local gate, CUDA 12.8.1 in Docker (`nvidia/cuda:12.8.1-devel-ubuntu24.04`), `nvcc -O3
-gencode=arch=compute_90a,code=sm_90a`; scripts and full output in
[evidence/2026-10-29-v1-d32-breakdown/](evidence/2026-10-29-v1-d32-breakdown/):

| build | result |
|---|---|
| sk, `SK_PDL=0` vs HEAD | 58 / 58 kernels SASS-identical (sk, wd, sgemv, rgemv); 0 spills; 0 C75xx |
| sk, `SK_PDL=1` vs HEAD | 26 single-CTA sk instances change, 32 identical; one `ACQBULK` (griddepcontrol.wait) added per changed kernel; registers equal (one 105 -> 103); 0 spills; 0 C75xx; SYNCS, BAR, MEMBAR, FENCE, UBLKCP, LDG/STG/LDS/STS counts unchanged; the two fused kernels drop one UTMALDG site (the router-piece and pooler x copies share `issue_x`) |
| attention, `ONE_TRIG=0` vs HEAD | 15 / 15 kernels SASS-identical (13 instances, both probes) |
| attention, `ONE_TRIG=1` vs HEAD | 8 instances add one `PREEXIT` (launch_dependents): <1,1>, <1,3>, <1,11>, <1,13>, <1,17>, <1,49>, <8,11>, <8,15>; modes 51, 115, 19, 3 and <8,1> SASS-identical; registers equal, 0 spills, 0 C75xx, every other sync and memory opcode count equal |

The device-only sk build needed one workaround that predates this change: the stale
`cgemv_launch` explicit instantiations in `sk_source(device_only=True)` no longer match the
reworked signature (since `a35709c`), so `gen_sk.py` drops them from both builds. The host
launch path (`cudaLaunchKernelEx` with the PDL attribute) was not compiled here; it mirrors the
cluster branch's call. Nothing ran on a GPU.
