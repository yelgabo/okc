## Two real deltas, ranked by decision value

### 1. Long dense library policy: concrete, multi-load, no demonstrated winning budget

V16/V32, both contexts, are the cleanest distinct complete-file policy difference: ordinary torch dense output versus accepted fixed Lt plans and common composition. Computation remains BF16-input GEMMs with appropriate FP32 accumulation/output and legal boundary conversion. O/combined-PR/full-unpool weights contain 16+72+64=**152 MiB logical BF16 weight bytes** per complete three-operation stream; actual HBM traffic may be much lower through caching. Remote's FP32 output tensors for these three stages total T*(8192+4608+8192)*4 = **10.25/20.5 MiB** for T128/256, before consumer reads. These are dataflow counts, not removable bytes or measured savings. A policy swap introduces no new expert ownership transfer; ordinary stream order supplies the producer/consumer relation, while all common publication/activation rules remain unchanged.

Published long profiles show about75/75us combined dense-library/reducer kernel durations at V16 sliding/full and about100/95us at V32. They are a material region, **not exposed-time evidence**: dispatch/reduction/dtype differences, overlap and profiler overhead prevent subtracting them from adjusted endpoint medians. The remote V16/V32 endpoint medians are263.548/348.392us and328.387/489.291us; owning607 is247.131/333.218us and316.390/482.657us. Those are unmatched jobs, not a causal Lt win, but they supply no positive endpoint reason to prefer this complete policy.

### 2. Broad PDL/unrepaired boundary chain: genuinely different, not a legal transplant
