## 2. Official rules and scoring, verbatim

### 2.1 Per-problem score (TASK.md, served by `GET /api/v1/problems/muse_decode_layer` as `task_md`; identical text in lilaclilac09's committed copy)

> A shape's weight is how many times one step runs it. A load's time is the sum of its shapes' times, each times its weight. Your speedup on the problem is the geometric mean, over loads, of SGLang's load time over yours, so each load counts equally. The time shown for a run is SGLang's time per step, averaged over loads the same way, divided by that speedup.

> The floor is the time to move the data at 4.8 TB/s or do the math at 989 TFLOP/s, whichever is longer.

Decode-layer scored shapes (TASK.md): six loads (decode 1/16/32, verify 1/16/32), each a sliding-window shape with weight 46 and a full-attention shape with weight 16, ctx 80000, page 1, "dist soft", "spread agentic" at batch 16/32. Floors in µs: decode 1 40.0/44.2, decode 16 76.7/137.5, decode 32 84.5/208.1, verify 1 62.3/66.4, verify 16 126.6/188.7, verify 32 143.8/267.2. Thirteen correctness tests run before timing (listed in TASK.md). bf16_proj: 30 shapes, every weight 62 (ozgureyilmaz's notes quoting TASK.md).

### 2.2 How a run is timed (TASK.md, prefill copy; lee101 quotes the same section from the MoE TASK.md with one extra sentence)

> Each shape is captured as a CUDA graph and timed by replaying it, in the same run and on the same GPU as SGLang's kernel, your replays and SGLang's taking turns on the same fresh inputs.
> Every timed replay starts with a cold L2: the judge reads a buffer larger than L2 just before it.
> A shape's speedup is the median of paired ratios: each repetition's SGLang time over yours, taken two repetitions at a time with the order swapped, so which side went first cancels. Your time on the shape is SGLang's median time over that speedup.

lee101 quotes the MoE TASK.md adding: "The judge subtracts a graph replay's fixed cost (about 4 µs) from both sides." Prefill TASK.md: "Prefill isn't part of a decode step, so this problem has its own board and doesn't count toward the overall board."

### 2.4 Validation, prizes, limits, dates, restrictions (llms.txt)

> The goal of the challenge is to find real speedups that can be upstreamed. This is the north star. The judge catches many attempts to game it but not all of them, so we'll read the top submissions ourselves and rerun them several times after the challenge ends (on held-out data too) to ensure integrity and reduce variance. Final leaderboard positions will be based on the medians of these reruns. All leaderboard positions are provisional until that review is done.

> Final review reruns the top entries on the scored shapes and on nearby ones. Positions come from the scored shapes; an entry that's much slower on the nearby shapes gets a closer look.

> $100,000 on the serving board: 1st $50,000 and a signed H200, 2nd $30,000, 3rd $10,000, 4th $3,000, 5th $2,000, 6th to 10th $1,000 each. How places are decided is under Validation.

> Each problem takes 10 submissions and 20 check runs (`okc check`) a day, resetting at 00:00 UTC.

> The kernel problems take submissions from Oct 22 at 11 am PT through Nov 4, and the serving track from Nov 5 at noon PT through Nov 19 (both close at 11:59 pm PT)

Restrictions (llms.txt, abridged to the load-bearing sentences): capturable in a CUDA graph; weights keep their precision ("NVFP4 weights stay NVFP4 and BF16 weights stay BF16, or smaller, losslessly. Environment: `lmsysorg/sglang:dev-muse-spark-open`, "PyTorch, Triton, FlashInfer, DeepGEMM, TileLang, CuTe DSL and CUTLASS"; `prepare()` untimed up to 300 s. Credits: "This challenge's judge implementation used GPU MODE's openly published reference-kernels and kernelbot as a starting point."

## 4. Public sources, techniques, and numbers

### 4.1 vjeux/okc-2026 and Madhavan113/kerneloptimizer (the strongest open code)

- https://github.com/vjeux/okc-2026 ("team vjeux", Apache 2.0, created 2026-10-27): five complete single-file CUDA submissions (attention 154 KB, proj 189 KB, layer 569 KB, MoE 179 KB, prefill 292 KB). The README states only the structure; the file docstrings describe the designs. Official API rows for vjeux: 1.501 / 2.610 / 2.327 / 2.102 / 1.798 (per-board ranks 169 / 39 / 72 / 75 / 39); Madhavan113: 1.493 / 2.588 / 2.332 / 2.101 / 1.796 (ranks 181 / 52 / 60 / 76 / 40).
- https://github.com/Madhavan113/kerneloptimizer: `notes/pub_moe_design.md`, `pub_attention_design.md`, `pub_proj_design.md` are line-referenced reverse-engineering memos of the vjeux files ("Reference design only: re-implement"), and `notes/decode_layer_map.md` and `notes/skx_streamk.md` are Madhavan113's own judged experiments. Madhavan113's stored runs (states): attention 2.5883x (job 26c40f28a3f74b2a), MoE 2.3318x (080c57e6047b4794), proj 1.4933x (8bd137632d7a4cc5), layer 2.0762x (711bbae5d04143b3) and later 2.1112x (b73aff83), prefill 1.7960x (45ca53d9449b4d00). The decode-layer map says the layer embeds the vjeux attention and MoE CUDA byte-for-byte and a forked proj module, so the two repos describe one kernel family (inferred from the memo's "BYTE-IDENTICAL" diffs).

MoE (`pub_moe_design.md`, memo header "2.32x vs SGLang Marlin on H200"; states):
- One kernel launch per call: routing prologue run redundantly by every CTA, GEMM1 + SwiGLU, GEMM2 + norm statistics, a software grid barrier, then the final norm and weighted sum. Grid = SM count (132), one CTA per SM, no PDL ("one launch per call gains nothing").
- Four compiled variants by M: TINY (M ≤ 4), TINY2 (5-8), MID (9-16): `mma.sync.m16n8k16` "skinny" units of 16 weight rows × full K with 8 compute warps splitting K (512 each) plus a helper warp for epilogues; MAIN (M ≥ 17): RS-wgmma `m64nNk16` with A = dequantized weights in registers, B = gathered x in shared memory, N = 8 × ceil(tokens/8) up to 64, 4 consumer warpgroups + 2 TMA producer warps, stream-K over 256-K stages with cost tables.
- Weights stream by TMA bulk copies of 36,864 B units (one 16-row × 4096-K block including scales), 3-slot ring; x goes global to registers for the skinny path. Hot experts: an expert with more than 8 tokens becomes ceil(tot/64) "fat" jobs of ≤ 64 tokens, listed first, largest first; in MID, experts with more than 8 tokens are split into ≤ 8-token jobs (weights re-streamed).
- Dequant: "7 ALU + 2 IMAD.SHL per 8 weights" (mask M = 0x81C081C0, shifts and funnel shifts chosen so a bit permutation done once in `prepare()` lands E2M1 bits in BF16 positions as value × 2^-126), scale built with PRMT + IMAD (+0x5C005C00) and applied with one HMUL2 per word; global scale in the epilogue as gs × 2^62.
- GEMM1 to GEMM2 chaining through global memory with `evict_last` hints; per-job readiness flags (`doneA`); in WGS the phase-A epilogue writes straight into phase-B's wgmma B layout. L2 prefetch of every x row (`prefetch.global.L2`) at kernel start; the first two unit TMAs are issued before routing ("off the cold-start critical path").
- Recorded deltas: "bs1 +2.5% / +3.5%" (FINPF), "bs8 +2.0..2.5%", stream-K cost table "bs32 +0.45, bs128 +0.94, bs256 +0.17%".

Attention (`pub_attention_design.md`, "check 2.58x vs SGLang FA3"; states):
- One launch per call, no memset, no second pass. Splits never exchange a running max: each split adds o·2^m and l·2^m into an FP32 workspace with `red.global.add.v4.f32` (QK-norm bounds the logits), and the last CTA (a ticket counting keys) or every CTA (cooperative finalize) normalizes, applies RMSNorm × sigmoid(gate), and zeroes what it read.
- Two kernel families: per-request split-KV, grid (n_splits, B, Hkv/2), for sliding windows and single requests; a "flat" kernel, grid (264,1,1) = 2 CTAs/SM, for full context at B > 1, where all requests' keys are split into equal shares with a per-boundary penalty (SEGK 400 for q1, 600 for q8: "b16 107.4 -> 103, b32 187.4 -> 184") and a producer warpgroup (setmaxnreg 56/200 at q1, 32/104 at q8) feeding a 3- or 4-stage mbarrier ring.
- One CTA = one request × one KV pair (16 query heads); a 128-byte cache row holds both KV heads and is fetched once as 8 × 16-byte `cp.async.cg`. 64-key tiles in 128B-swizzled smem that doubles as the wgmma B descriptor.
- q1: f16 `mma.sync` with E4M3 → f16 converted in registers (no FP8 MMA). q8: FP8 wgmma for QK^T (m64n64k32) and PV (m64n72k32) with eight rows of E4M3 ones in V^T that produce the row sums for free; lazy softmax offset checked with a bit test on packed P bytes.
- Latency work on sliding shapes: seq_len speculated from the page-table width at B = 1 and verified after the index group lands (SPEC), K/V issued as soon as page indices land, straight-line predicated copies, magic-multiplier division, cooperative finalize. Per-shape plans: decode 1 sliding 32 CTAs with one 64-key tile each ("6.6 -> 6.5 us vs 32" splits), verify 1 sliding 33 CTAs ("coop beats clusters 13.0 -> 10.2 us"), verify 32 sliding "4 splits per request, 128 CTAs one per SM, 16.10 -> 15.50", full B = 1 252 splits with 8 workspace copies ("coop with 1/2/4 copies +26/+16/+4%: same-address red serialization of 250 splits").
- Things that lost: "FMA-pipe exponentials", "two streams per CTA", "sense-reversing barriers", TMA for q8 page indices (+0.7%), PDL on the standalone (~0.3% worse).

Projections (`pub_proj_design.md`, "1.50x vs SGLang"; states):
- Lossless 12-bit weights for m ≤ 32: low byte raw, 4-bit code for the high byte as an offset in an 8-value exponent window chosen per matrix; escapes (weights outside the window) applied by dedicated escape warps from a per-CTA table while the weights stream; decode is about 12 integer ops per 8 weights.
- `sk2` kernel for m = 8/16/32: x is the mma A operand (16 tokens), weights the B operand (8 rows); no cross-CTA split-K (K is split only across warps inside the CTA); weights by one bulk copy per stage (12-24 KB), x by a 3-D TMA box; 96-256 CTAs, 3-6 stages, 64-213 KB smem per config. The router at m ≤ 32 uses a 3-CTA cluster with a DSMEM reduction.
- m = 1 is autotuned among a 12-bit CUDA-core GEMV (`cgemv`, "pooler m1 2x512 -0.34 us vs sk2"), `sgemv`, `rgemv` (FP32 router), `sk2`, cuBLAS. m = 128/256 never uses 12-bit ("the wide (RS-wgmma) kernel never beats cuBLAS for BF16 m 33..256"): cuBLAS, cuBLAS on a K-major copy, cluster split-K kernels `gq`/`gqx` ("cuBLAS 16.4 -> 14.0 us" at qkv m128), CUTLASS pingpong 128x64x64 for pooler m256 ("31.3 vs 34.5 raw").
- `prepare()` autotunes every (layer, m) with CUDA-graph capture, a 256 MB cold-L2 sweep before each replay, 24 interleaved reps, and an FP64 self-test gate.

Decode layer (`decode_layer_map.md`; states, judged 2.092x at job f662ee18, later 2.1112x):
- At T ≤ 32, nine launches, all with PDL: qkv `sk2` → attention → o `sk2` → `_boundary1` (Triton, one program per token) → fused pooler + router `sk2f` (T 1/8/16; separate at T = 32) → `_route_split` → `moe_fused` (one launch) → unpooler `sk2` → `_boundary2`. At T > 32 (verify 16/32) cuBLAS for every dense GEMM, pooler + router as one `torch.mm` on a concatenated [4608 × 8192] weight, no PDL ("-1% at T = 256").
- Budget at decode 1 sliding (judged 77.1 µs): attention 4.9, MoE 36.7, dense GEMMs 39.3 (qkv 10.1 / o 5.8 / pooler+router 17.7 / unpooler 5.7), glue 13.2 (three Triton kernels, "8-17% of the judged time").
- Negative results, all judged: fusing the glue into a last-CTA tail of the GEMV ("o launch 9.0 -> 49.2 us", "LATENCY-BOUND"); a boundary prologue recomputing the norm in every CTA (+13.3 µs at T = 1); "under PDL a kernel must not end with a grid barrier (it blocks the dependent's early start: 3-5 us)"; "PDL depth 2 = 3 = 4 = 6 at every T"; routing fused into the pooler kernel (2.051, regression); the standalone proj module composed into the layer lost on every T ≤ 32 shape; attention `-DOKC_PDLKV=1` +0.4..+1.1 µs. "MoE and attention kernel times move 5-20 us run to run on identical code" because the judge's fresh routing differs per run.
- `skx_streamk.md`: a persistent stream-K wgmma GEMM for m = 128/256 lost to cuBLAS/`gqx` in four judged checks (1.4756, 1.4620, 1.4781, 1.4882 vs 1.4933 reference). Attribution: the main loop alone beats the picks by 1.2-1.6 µs (W streams at 4.1-4.2 TB/s plus ~2 µs ramp), and any global fixup costs 9-11 µs. "No stream-K / global-fixup form wins at m=128 on this GPU; the gqx / gq cluster-DSMEM forms win precisely because they have no global fixup."
- `codex-research-handoff.md` lists what the team considers already done (persistent execution, TMA, WGMMA, stream-K, lossless packing, PDL) and the remaining bets (a verify-GEMM decomposition experiment, distributing paged-KV address math across lanes, MoE weight-stream dependencies, Cohere-style dependency-driven persistent layer as a research branch).

## 6. Cross-source synthesis per topic

Decode-layer structure. Nobody shipped a megakernel: vjeux/Madhavan113 run nine PDL-chained launches at T ≤ 32 (states), lee101 11 per load (states), infraax 16-20 (states), operatorstack keeps SGLang's structure with per-token-count kernel swaps (states); infraax lists a "single-launch megakernel" among closed ideas and Madhavan113 lists Cohere's megakernel as a research branch, so a true megakernel is unproven here (inferred). Fusing the small glue kernels into neighbouring GEMVs lost in three independent attempts (Madhavan113 last-CTA tails, lee101 v31 epilogues, infraax grid-barrier fusion) and the one measured glue win is a side stream for the router over the pooler (SGLang's own structure). The MoE is the largest block at every decode shape and the attention at full-context batch 16/32 (every profile agrees).

MoE GEMM. Every open design puts weights on the MMA M side (register A operand after in-register dequant) and tokens on N: vjeux `mma.sync` 16-row units with K split across 8 warps for ≤ 16 tokens and RS-wgmma `m64nNk16` with N ≤ 64 and stream-K above (states); lee101 persistent `mma.sync` with TMA rings and heaviest-first queue (states); operatorstack `mma_v2` register-A with per-item 8/16/32-row tiles (states); a wgmma shared-B path "was off, because the register path was faster" (operatorstack states). Dequant cost is the agreed lever: Marlin 15+4 ops per 8 values, vjeux 7+2 integer plus 4 HMUL2, lee101 8 integer plus 4 HMUL2 (states); lee101 and arkintof both report that cutting instructions without keeping occupancy gains nothing (states). Hot experts are handled by chunking (vjeux fat jobs ≤ 64 tokens; operatorstack 32-row items; Ranoobaba Marlin block 16) rather than special kernels; arkintof's hot/cold split was flat (states). Warp-specialised producer/consumer rings lost for lee101 (v6) and infraax (three readings) (states). The leader's 2.62-2.69 is consistent with ~92% of the weight-streaming floor on every load (lee101's inference from floors).

Attention. All open kernels gather page-size-1 FP8 rows as 128-byte rows with 8 lanes × 16 B `cp.async` (vjeux, operatorstack, lee101 states); per-row bulk TMA and one-row-per-thread lose badly (lee101, emeraldant states). Split-KV with a merge: vjeux merges in an absolute frame with FP32 `red.add` and a key-count ticket (states); operatorstack and infraax merge with log-sum-exp by the last program (states). q8 verify blocks use 64-row wgmma tiles (8 tokens × 8 heads) with FP8 QK and FP8 PV (vjeux, lee101 states; operatorstack uses `warpgroup_mma`). Batch-1 sliding shapes are latency chains of 5-6 dependent DRAM trips (Ranoobaba, lee101 states); the fixes that measured were seq-length speculation, issuing K/V as soon as indices land, one 64-key tile per CTA, cooperative finalize (vjeux), and 16-CTA clusters launched with PDL (lee101 s1k 7.2 µs at decode-1 sliding). Clusters larger than 2-4 CTAs fail on GPC packing for big grids (lee101 states). exp2/MUFU is not the verify bottleneck (infraax measured 7%); BN 128 tiles at batch ≥ 16 gave ×1.13-1.16 (infraax states).

Projections. The leaders are inferred (lee101) to sit within ~1.5 µs of a lossless-compressed streaming floor on m ≤ 32 and to beat cuBLAS at m128 with compressed weights; the open code does this with 12-bit lossless weights (vjeux, lee101), swap-AB skinny kernels with no cross-CTA split-K (vjeux sk2), DSMEM cluster reductions where split-K is needed (vjeux router, operatorstack qkv/unpooler), CUDA-core GEMVs at m = 1 (vjeux, operatorstack, bra1nDump), and cuBLAS/CUTLASS at m ≥ 128 (vjeux, Madhavan113, operatorstack). Global split-K fixups cost 4-11 µs and lost everywhere they were tried at m = 128 (Madhavan113 skx, tommyyliu, ozgureyilmaz v028). The router hi/lo split with FP32 accumulation is universally used above 8 tokens; exact FP32 register-accumulated GEMVs win at m ≤ 8 (ozgureyilmaz, vjeux rgemv).

Small-batch latency tricks. PDL: vjeux's layer launches everything with PDL at T ≤ 32 (states); operatorstack chains router partials → reduce and prefetches weights into L2 before `griddepcontrol.wait` (states); bra1nDump measured +4-9% on router split-K pairs (states); infraax measured PDL as noise on Triton kernels, light prefetch +2 µs and full prefetch −3.9 µs at T = 1 (states); Madhavan113 found PDL depth irrelevant and a grid barrier at the end of a PDL-chained kernel costing 3-5 µs (states). L2 prefetch of activations (vjeux XPF2) and issuing the first weight TMAs before routing (vjeux FAST1) measured +2.5-3.5% at bs1 (states); L2 prefetch of KV ahead of the ring hurts (lee101, infraax states). Streams: a side stream for router + routing overlapping the pooler (SGLang baseline, operatorstack, vjeux prefill) is the only stream overlap reported to help; two streams per attention CTA lost (vjeux states). Judge arithmetic that matters for latency: ~4 µs replay cost subtracted, kernels plus inter-kernel gaps of 0.3-0.8 µs are scored, ~2-2.5 µs extra non-kernel gap on cuBLAS-served shapes, and ~3% run-to-run drift (infraax, prateekm1007 states).
