# Gap map of the decode layer, 2026-10-29 (after 1.878x)

Read-only analysis of main at fc2cea6. No code changed and no check was run for it. It
replaces the per-load table in [plan-2026-10-29-steering.md](plan-2026-10-29-steering.md),
which was computed on the 1.782x file.

## Inputs and method

- **Ours.** Three judge runs of the same timed code: check 725d34e9511c4370 (1.894x), its
  repeat 80b98f567aa74dd0 (1.873x), and the scored run 22d5710c4e8540d6 (1.878x, the file of
  80b98f56). Per-shape medians and profile rows come from
  `muse_decode_layer/profiles/submission.<job>.json`; `okc status 22d5710c4e8540d6 --json`
  agrees with the profile JSON (status ok, 13/13 tests). Where a number is a mean, it is the
  mean over these three runs.
- **Public.** The judged in-layer profile of the public entry's job f662ee18 (2.092x), as
  tabulated in [gap-analysis-2026-10-28.md](gap-analysis-2026-10-28.md) section 2. Its
  component rows are attention, MoE, dense (qkv, o, pooler with router, unpooler) and glue
  (three Triton kernels: two boundaries and a route kernel that also does the pre-expert norm).
- **Score math.** Per load, C = 46 t_sliding + 16 t_full. The score is the geometric mean of
  the six ratios C_sglang / C_ours. A load's share of the remaining gap is
  ln(C_ours / C_public) over the sum of that log across the six loads. Closing a load
  completely lifts the score by ln(C_ours / C_public) / 6. The same script on a41ace0b94ba4369
  reproduces the steering plan's table to the decimal, so the two tables are comparable.
- **Cross-run caveat.** The public times come from a different judge run, and absolute times
  drift between runs. SGLang's own verify 32 sliding time was 747.4, 728.9 and 690.9 us in
  our three runs, and our time on that shape moved 385.9, 385.6 and 372.8. Comparisons against
  the public entry are therefore hypothesis-level, about plus or minus 3-5% per shape.
  Comparisons between our own runs use within-run ratios.
- **PDL caveat.** Under programmatic dependent launch a secondary kernel's profile row
  includes the time it waits on its predecessor. The public layer chains every launch this
  way, so its rows overstate the later kernels and its sum of rows exceeds its judged time by
  5-39 us. I report a residual column (judged time minus the sum of rows) on both sides. Only
  component plus residual is a like-for-like comparison.

## 3. Three experiments by expected value

Expected value here is predicted score gain times the chance the mechanism is real, over the
cost of the cheapest decisive test. Savings are hypotheses unless marked measured. Score
percentages use this map's three-run mean load times.

### E1. Take the MoE job derivation off the critical path at 128 and 256 tokens

- **Bottleneck (measured, standalone).** In mode 16 stamps (9332e2a49aff4ff2) the routing and
  x gather run before any consumer starts and take 14.4-15.2 us mean (18-19 max) at 128 tokens
  and 19.3-20.4 (23-27 max) at 256. The stamps add 14-18 us per launch in total, so the true
  prologue is shorter. Mode 23 keeps this prologue. In the layer the fused kernel "routes on
  the device from the global ids" (`_route_meta`), so it rebuilds per-expert counts, the job
  list and S.ord from topk_ids after the side stream joins.
- **Mechanism.** Build the job table (per-expert route counts, jobs, S.ord, the x gather
  index) in a small kernel on the side stream right after SGLang's routing kernel. That side
  stream is idle under the concatenated pooler + router GEMM (26 us at 128 tokens, 33 at 256)
  and today carries only the routing kernel and the hi+lo add (5 us). The fat kernel then
  reads a finished table, its weights producer issues the first units at entry, and only the
  x gather stays ahead of the consumers. Ordering obligations are the usual cross-stream ones,
  already met by the event join. No fence inside the kernel changes; the table is written
  before the join and read after it.
- **Predicted saving (hypothesis).** 6-12 us per shape at 128 and 256 tokens, the prologue
  minus the gather and the stamp overhead. Score +0.5% to +1.0% (verify 16 +0.3 to +0.6,
  verify 32 +0.2 to +0.5), more if decode 32 runs the fat path.
- **Why ruled-out.md does not close it.** The closed MoE rows are about partitioning (cost
  model, least-squares fit, static phase B ranges), splitting GEMM1 and GEMM2 into two
  launches (staged pair), per-unit credits and weight load paths. None of them moves routing
  work. The layer's closed prefetch rows warm L2 with weights on the side stream; E1 moves
  index work, not bytes. The PDL row closes launch latency, and the prologue is in-kernel
  serial work, not launch latency.
- **Cheapest decisive test.** One standalone MoE check with a timing-only arm of mode 23 that
  receives the job table precomputed on the host side of the bench (the existing no-gate
  diagnostic is the pattern), paired in one process against mode 23 on judge-like routing,
  six seeds each at 128 and 256 tokens. Stop if the arm saves less than 4 us at 128 tokens.
  Adding the public MoE file as a third arm, loaded from the session scratchpad and not
  committed (as bench d57d1fe064164611 did for attention), would also measure the whole
  same-process MoE gap and tell whether E1 can matter relative to it.

### E2. FP8 P.V on wgmma in the full-context q_len 8 kernel (mode 51)

- **Bottleneck (measured, same process).** Verify 16 / 32 full attention is +23.6 / +53.4 us
  against the public file (d57d1fe064164611, warm). In the layer it is +12.8 / +40.8 us
  cross-run.
- **Mechanism.** Our q_len 8 path rounds P to f16 and runs P.V on f16 wgmma with V converted
  to f16 by the loader warps (about 800 cycles per tile, gap analysis 3.1). The public kernel
  runs P.V on e4m3 wgmma, transposes V in shared memory with `ldmatrix.trans`, `prmt` and
  `stmatrix`, and appends eight rows of e4m3 ones to V^T so the same wgmma produces the row
  sums. That removes the conversion, halves the B-operand bytes per tile and removes the
  separate row-sum reduction. P rounded to e4m3 is what FA3's FP8 path does, which the
  tolerance (2.5x the error of the reference with FP8-rounded queries) is built around.
- **Predicted saving (hypothesis).** The gap analysis puts the cost of our more precise P at
  about 8% of full verify attention: about 9 us at verify 16 full and 18 us at verify 32 full.
  Score +0.3% on the layer (verify 32 +0.18, verify 16 +0.12), and the same shapes count on
  the attention board. The measured ceiling for all of the full verify attention gap is
  +0.8%.
- **Why ruled-out.md does not close it.** The FP8 row closes FP8 on mma.sync, which compiles
  to F2FP + HMMA on H200; this is wgmma, which runs e4m3 natively. The mode 51 rows close the
  spilling build, the per-request order (QKF), early stage release and the once-path diet.
  None of them touches the P.V operand type.
- **Cheapest decisive test.** One attention bench check with `tools/attn_make_bench_ab.py`: an
  FP8 P.V arm of mode 51 against the board build at verify 16 and 32 full, warm and cold,
  paired, with ABCHECK error against the FP32 reference. Stop if the warm saving at verify 32
  full is under 5 us, or if the worst error uses more than half of the tolerance margin on
  any of the 16 tests.

### E3. Attention boundary at 128 and 256 tokens

- **Bottleneck (measured, cross-run but consistent in all three runs).** Our attention
  boundary takes 10.0-10.6 us at 128 tokens and 15.3-16.3 us at 256. SGLang's kernel of the
  same class takes 4.8-5.0 / 7.1-7.8 us for the FFN boundary in the same profiles, and SGLang's
  own attention boundary took 5.0 / 7.5-8.6 us in its baseline profile. At 16 and 32 tokens
  ours is at parity.
- **Mechanism (hypothesis).** Our kernel uses a cluster of 8 CTAs per token with DSMEM
  reductions. At 128 and 256 tokens that is 1,024 and 2,048 CTAs in 8-CTA clusters, and
  8-CTA clusters pack poorly onto GPCs (public notes and our attention evidence). SGLang
  switches to a different block shape above 16 tokens, which our self-test log notes. A
  second possible cause: the row includes a PDL wait on the cuBLAS o projection; the positive
  residual on verify 16 sliding argues against that.
- **Predicted saving.** 5.2 us per verify 16 shape and 7.7 per verify 32 shape if SGLang's
  kernel replaces ours above 32 tokens. Score +0.55% (verify 16 +0.26, verify 32 +0.29).
- **Why ruled-out.md does not close it.** The only boundary row is "X5 boundary, one block
  per token", the FFN boundary at 8 tokens. The attention boundary has no loser row, and its
  introduction check (9cb961d383ab42be) was judged on decode 1 only; its verify 16 / 32
  deltas were inside the SGLang drift of that run.
- **Cheapest decisive test.** A token threshold on `_abnd` (ours at 32 tokens and below,
  SGLang's module above), timed in prepare as a chain (o GEMM then boundary, graph replays,
  six paired samples per size, the way `_front_tune` times front variants), with the pick
  printed in SUMMARY. One check. Keep it if both sizes save at least 3 us in the chain and the
  verify 16 / 32 within-run ratios are not below the three-run range above.

### Not chosen, with reasons

- **Pinning sk8w4s at 32 tokens.** The judged ratios do not separate the two picks (section
  2), so the expected gain is under the noise. A paired in-layer chain A/B at 32 tokens with
  more seeds would decide it cheaply, but it does not rank above E1-E3.
- **The residual between kernels.** It is +2.3% of score, but the PDL row in ruled-out.md
  shows our chain did not shrink under PDL, and the public residual is distorted by its own
  PDL rows. Only fusion that removes a launch can recover it, and no candidate fusion has
  evidence yet.
- **Dense projections.** At parity at 16 tokens and above, and within 1-3 us at 1 and 32
  tokens.
