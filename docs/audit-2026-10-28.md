## 5. Component findings

Five audits ran in parallel on 2026-10-28 (layer orchestration, MoE, attention, projections,
public entries); their full reports are in this session's scratchpad under `audit/`. Score
deltas use the sensitivities in section 2 at today's operating point. The companion document
`docs/gap-analysis-2026-10-28.md` compares every component with the public 2.1x entry.

### 5.1 Layer orchestration (+0.10 to +0.12 available, 1-2 days)

- Launches: 17 at decode 1 sliding, 20-23 elsewhere, against 9 in the public 2.1x layer. PDL
  exists only inside the W4 chain; none of our proj, attention, router or boundary kernels
  triggers `griddepcontrol.launch_dependents`, so SGLang's own PDL-secondary kernels (the two
  boundaries, routing) gain nothing after ours. Idle from exposed launch boundaries: 10-20 us
  at T <= 8, 11-24 us above (launch-count model; the profiler cannot resolve it).
- SGLang's boundary kernel (`norm_residual.cuh` at a55ab7d5, verified today) rounds to BF16
  at exactly two points: the input-scale product before the branch RMS (FFN side) and the
  normalized branch before the residual add; the stream stays FP32 and the router copy is
  unrounded. X5 skips both; two `bf16r()` calls make it SGLang's math. The X5 hold is lifted.
- Ranked: (1) own pre-expert norm kernel with PDL, torch's `F.rms_norm` costs 6 us on all 12
  shapes: +0.033, 2-3 h; later fold it into GEMM1's activation staging: +0.009. (2) X5 with
  SGLang's rounding: +0.006 to +0.011, 1 h. (3) PDL across our chain (qkv -> attention -> o ->
  boundary -> pooler; finalize -> unpooler -> boundary) with weight prologues before the wait:
  about +0.03, 4-6 h; the trap is any read of the predecessor's output above the wait.
  (4) L2 prefetch of the o, router and unpooler-slice weights (34 MB) on the side stream while
  attention runs at T <= 8: +0.019 (up to +0.039 with the T >= 16 shapes), 3-4 h, medium-low
  confidence until the cache policy is measured. (5) Own attention boundary with PDL on both
  sides: +0.013. (6) After (1) the side chain is co-critical at T >= 8: `PORT_ROUTER_MMA` at
  T >= 16, routing + route_align fused, and at T > 32 pooler + router hi + router lo as one
  concatenated cuBLAS GEMM as the public layer does (our router costs 43 us of rows at verify
  32): +0.01 to +0.02.

### 5.2 MoE (+0.13 to +0.16 available on the existing kernel; the public design is worth about +0.25)

- At 128 and 256 tokens the W4 GEMMs are bound by L2-to-SM traffic, not HBM: at T = 256 each
  unique weight byte crosses L2 to SM 1.5x (38 items for 25 experts) plus activation staging
  and partial writes, about 960 MB for 472 MB of weights at 4.1-4.5 TB/s. The decisive evidence
  was already in `docs/runs-moe.md`: skewed routings with the same item count and 6x fewer
  unique bytes are not faster. The model `t = max(SM-side bytes / 4.3 TB/s, unique / 3.9)`
  fits all four big kernels within 10%.
- mma.sync cannot take large token tiles: with weights as A fragments in registers and tokens
  as B through `ldmatrix`, NT = 8 saturates the 128 B/cycle shared-memory port. That is why
  the 64-route tiles (M1) lost 2x on normal routing. Register-A `wgmma` (A from registers, B
  through a descriptor) is what the public kernel uses for jobs of up to 64 tokens.
- At T = 1 the GEMMs are within 5-6 us of streaming; the rest is three dependent launch hops
  (2-3 us each) and each grid's fill and drain. The 12-17 us SwiGLU and finalize rows in the
  profile are PDL wait, not cost.
- Ranked on the existing kernel: P1 hot-expert wgmma kernel (dequant once per CTA into
  swizzled smem, two consumer warpgroups, cluster-of-4 multicast of the token slab; experts with
  more than 16 routes): +0.055 to +0.075, 5-6 days, medium; kill test E1 (2 h) first. P2 SwiGLU
  in GEMM1's last-split epilogue and bf16 y from GEMM2: +0.02, 1.5 days. P5 persistent fused
  GEMM1 + SwiGLU + GEMM2 at T <= 32: +0.025 to +0.04, 3-4 days. P4 T = 1 configs W8 D8 and
  S = 1 D8: +0.004 to +0.008, 1 h. P6 dense block indexing (41K launched vs 9.6K real blocks at
  T = 256): 0 to +0.02, 1 h. P7 route_align in GEMM1's prologue: +0.004.
- The public kernel's MoE rows in the layer are 60 / 86 us at decode 16 / 32 sliding and
  163 / 198 at verify 16 / 32 against our 112 / 120 and 246 / 381: one persistent launch,
  36 KB bulk-copied weight units consumed through registers, x in registers, 7 ALU + 2 IMAD
  dequant per word, register-A wgmma with N up to 64 and stream-K above 16 tokens.

### 5.3 Attention (+0.10 to +0.12 available on the existing kernels; the public design is worth about +0.11 to +0.15)

- Sliding shapes are bound by fixed per-launch cost. The q_len 1 cluster kernel at batch 1
  spends 7.0k cycles in its prologue (three dependent cold round trips plus a q-row, inv_freq,
  `sincosf` chain), 3.3k on the last split's new-token tile that every CTA waits for, 5.9k in a
  merge that does 1k cycles of arithmetic, against 3.3k for its data tiles. The local sm_90a
  compile shows 98.6 KB of SASS with 17 inlined `sincosf` copies and a 10.8 KB straight-line
  merge; the ws kernel is 85 KB with 20 copies and a second launch worth 3.3 us plus a gap.
- Full context: the ws kernel is coupled at 1.73k cycles per tile on the loader and math sides;
  the MUFU/F2FP pipe is 89% subscribed at the gather ceiling (ex2 512 + P pack 256 + V convert
  256 cycles per tile). Plain 1-D `cp.async.bulk` cannot feed wgmma's e4m3 K operand (layout
  proof in the report); the viable form is tensor-map TMA with a one-row box (backlog A4). The
  fused q_len 1 kernel's full-context loop runs 4.7k cycles per tile against the ws loaders'
  1.1-1.7k.
- Ranked: R1 q_len 1 sliding diet (hoist cold loads, one `sincosf` body, new token under tile
  0, about 20 KB of code): d1s 14.5 -> 8-9, +0.017, 6-10 h. R2 q_len 1 full on ws-style loaders
  with one consumer warpgroup and two CTAs per SM: d32f 254 -> 190-200, +0.030, 16-24 h. R3
  q_len 8 full: row TMA + FP8 P.V with PRMT-transposed V + part of the exponentials on the FMA
  pipe: v32f 354 -> 230-260, +0.019, 20-28 h. R4 q_len 8 sliding in-kernel cluster merge:
  +0.013, 10-16 h. R5 layer PDL overlap: +0.02, 6-10 h.
- The public kernel reaches 4.9 us at decode 1 sliding and 170 / 186 us at decode / verify 32
  full with one launch: a fixed-frame FP32 atomic merge (the QK-norm bound makes it exact),
  one 64-key tile per CTA at batch 1, the sequence length speculated from the page table's
  width, a flat 264-CTA full-context kernel with equal key shares, FP8 wgmma P.V with ones-rows
  for the row sums. See the gap analysis, sections 3.1 and 5.

### 5.4 Dense projections (+0.04 to +0.07 on BF16 bytes; compression is the public entry's extra)

- Calibration: `docs/runs-moe.md:24` measured the judge's streaming line, t = 1.3 us +
  bytes / 4.05 TB/s. Our m = 1 kernels sit on it (pooler 18.2 vs 17.9 modelled, qkv 10.2 vs
  10.6, o 4.7 vs 5.4); the "65-72% of peak" figure in section 3 is the judge's per-shape time
  including 1.2-2.2 us of launch overhead. A new GEMV load path cannot buy more than about
  0.7 us unless it moves fewer bytes. The public entry's 12-bit lossless weights with escapes
  applied by dedicated warps are how its pooler takes 13.7 us at m = 1 against our 18.2.
- Above 8 tokens cuBLAS's unpooler and o kernels are within 0.6 us of the line; the slack is
  in the pooler, qkv (two launches) and everything at m = 128 / 256. The public entry's
  no-reduction swap-AB kernel (x as the mma A operand, 8-row weight tiles as B, K split only
  across warps) takes the dense block from our 74 / 77 us to 51 / 60 at decode 16 / 32; at
  m >= 128 it found nothing beyond about 1 us over cuBLAS except a cluster split-K qkv kernel
  (16.4 -> 14.0) and CUTLASS for the pooler at m = 256.
- Ranked: (1) skinny TMA + wgmma GEMM for 9 <= T <= 256 with the hi/lo router on the same
  kernel: +0.025 to +0.048, 36-50 h, medium (medium-low at m = 256); go/no-go experiment E3
  at m = 16 / 32. (2) PDL weight prefetch on every projection and router launch: +0.013 to
  +0.026, 3-4 h (overlaps 5.1 item 3). (3) the same kernel at N = 8 for T <= 8, gated by a
  bulk-copy streaming experiment E1. (4) the FP32 router at T = 8 is starved under the pooler,
  not slow; about 0 in the layer.
- Not in the audit's list but in the public entry: 12-bit weights with escape warps (reopens
  the compression entry; see `docs/ruled-out.md`), a fused pooler + router launch at T <= 16,
  and the m = 1 CUDA-core GEMV on 12-bit weights.
